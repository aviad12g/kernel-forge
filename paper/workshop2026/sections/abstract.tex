LLM systems generate multiple GPU kernels and often promote the fastest measured
candidate, coupling search to evaluation. Timing noise, candidate multiplicity,
or evaluator lifecycle errors can then become performance claims. We present
\system{}, a contract-aware protocol that separates screening from independent
confirmation using persistent modules, randomized paired CUDA-event blocks,
fresh processes, formal controls, and a checksummed artifact ledger. On a
performance-blind subset of 48 official KernelBench L1 tasks, 27 of 141 evaluated
Gemini candidates passed the full gate, covering 10 tasks; none of the frozen
task winners exceeded eager by the prespecified 2\% margin in either screening
or seven-process confirmation. A deterministic fused8 grid first supplied a
negative control: all 80 easy-regime candidates remained above the margin. We
then calibrated a disjoint 32-candidate stress test near parity. At budget
$K=8$, three of four tasks appeared above the margin in screening, but only two
confirmed; the median log optimism was 0.0072 with four-task bootstrap interval
$[-0.0124,0.0266]$. Thus the campaign observes one false promotion while not
supporting a population effect. A lifecycle ablation separately measured
1.053$\times$ synchronized-host inflation with no corresponding device-time
change. These results support independent confirmation as a useful default. We
make no full-KernelBench or state-of-the-art claim.
